# New stage music prompts

2026-11-02. Original Suno briefs, based on the current stage designs. These are creative targets, not guaranteed generator settings. Select Instrumental. BPM is a requested target. A generated track still needs listening, trimming and a loop crossfade before integration; asking for a loop does not guarantee sample-accurate looping.

Keep a shared restrained electronic pulse and occasional glassy echo across Chip Arena, but give each stage its own rhythm, instrumentation and melodic contour. Avoid putting the same dark-EDM/boss-battle template into every prompt. Keep musical effects distinct from actual hazard warning cues.

## Breakwater — Last Light Duel

Instrumental arena combat soundtrack, 128 BPM, blue-hour lighthouse duel on a rain-dark stone pier. Tense melodic breakbeat, tight dry drums, rounded pulsing analog bass, a memorable descending four-note muted-pluck melody, distant airy guitar harmonics, restrained cinematic strings. Spacious, focused, agile, controlled competitive energy. Alternate nimble percussion passages with wider oceanic synth chords; keep the groove moving throughout. Short rhythmic opening, recurring motif with variations, end on the opening groove for loop editing. No vocals, choir, foghorns, huge EDM drops or triumphant finale.

## Pier 9 — Midnight Freight

Instrumental combat soundtrack, 112 BPM, a sprawling container port under sodium floodlights at midnight. Heavy industrial electro with a lurching syncopated groove, deep rubbery bass, sampled metallic knocks, chain-like percussion, clipped low brass and a terse three-note baritone synth hook. Dirty, mechanical, urban, predatory. Trade spacious bass-and-percussion sections with densely interlocking machine rhythms; maintain forward pressure without a long breakdown. Immediate groove, circular arrangement, open rhythmic ending for loop editing. No vocals, alarm beeps, sirens, rock guitars or festival EDM risers.

## Skyframe — Above the Skyline

Instrumental high-altitude arena combat, 168 BPM, fighting across exposed steel floors of an unfinished skyscraper in bright daylight. Kinetic drum and bass with crisp chopped breaks, rapid ascending arpeggios, agile orchestral spiccato strings, clean sub-bass and bright suspended synth chords. Exhilarating height, wind, precision, danger and momentum. A distinctive rising five-note motif answers sharp rhythmic stops; shift between airy half-time space and full-speed percussion while retaining a clear pulse. Brief opening, evolving repeating sections, return to the opening rhythm. No vocals, horror drones, overdriven wall-of-sound bass or final victory cadence.

## Deepwell — Pressure Below

Instrumental storm-platform combat soundtrack, 96 BPM, an isolated offshore drilling rig battered by black seas. Crushing half-time industrial bass, asymmetrical tom patterns, bowed metal textures, low cello ostinato, distorted bass pulses and a slow descending chromatic motif. Claustrophobic, massive, volatile; sudden percussion gaps followed by heavy syncopated returns. Let restrained high string tremolo suggest wind above the machinery. Escalate through rhythmic layering rather than constant loudness. Start with the core pulse, finish unresolved over that same pulse for loop editing. No vocals, actual thunder strikes, sirens, generic trailer booms or soaring heroic melody.

## Polaris Station — Aurora Protocol

Instrumental polar research-station combat, 138 BPM, snow-lit buildings beneath a green-violet aurora. Cold hypnotic electro, intricate ticking percussion, tight kick and sub-bass, crystalline FM bells, evolving granular pads and sparse bowed string harmonics. A searching six-note bell phrase with wide intervals, luminous but isolated and uneasy. Alternate precise stalking rhythm with flowing aurora-like counter-melodies; sustain combat momentum with plenty of space for game audio. Short opening, restrained evolving arrangement, return to the initial pulse without resolving. No vocals, sleigh bells, Christmas harmony, huge dubstep drops or sentimental piano ballad.

## Backrooms Level 0 — Fluorescent Memory

Instrumental liminal-horror game underscore, 84 BPM, endless low-ceiling yellow offices with damp carpet and fluorescent lights. Oppressive dark ambient with a barely audible irregular mechanical pulse, detuned electric piano fragments, filtered tape hiss, soft electrical drone, muted sub-bass and a recurring two-note motif whose second note arrives slightly late. Familiar office calm becoming quietly wrong. Slow variations, small dissonant shifts and empty spaces; tension through repetition rather than loudness. Keep percussion minimal and distant; maintain a restrained pulse during encounters. Begin and end inside the same unresolved texture for loop editing. No vocals, screams, jump scares, EDM drops, heroic strings or actual gameplay warning sounds.

## Backrooms Poolrooms — Beneath Still Water

Instrumental aquatic liminal-horror soundtrack, 72 BPM, enormous empty white-tiled pools under soft diffuse light. Weightless ambient dub, submerged rounded bass pulses, hollow ceramic taps, watery FM plucks, reversed vibraphone swells and widely spaced shimmering chords with one quietly dissonant note. Beautiful, dreamlike and increasingly uncanny. Long tiled-hall reverberation around a sparse descending three-note motif; gentle drifting rhythm, occasional distant low pressure tones, no explosive climax. Different from a dirty industrial corridor: clean, vast, watery and unnervingly serene. Begin and end on the same suspended harmony for loop editing. No vocals, beach music, tropical instruments, literal splashes, jump scares or aggressive EDM drums.

Poolrooms is a future music brief: current shared source has no playable Poolrooms map, so it cannot be enabled in Random yet.

## Calibration Bay — Systems Ready

Instrumental training-room background music, 104 BPM. Minimal futuristic downtempo, clean soft percussion, warm steady bass, quiet modular synth sequences and a short neutral four-note motif. Clinical but comfortable, focused, understated, suitable for long weapon-testing sessions. Small variations every few phrases, consistent dynamics, spacious mix with no distracting transients. Start directly in the groove and return to it at the end for loop editing. No vocals, combat crescendos, dramatic horror, alarm sounds or big drops.

Calibration Bay remains a training-only sandbox and does not enter PvP Random.

## The 2026-11-10 stages

Four briefs for the owner's Suno songs, one per new stage, in the same spirit as the ones above. Each stage plays a
placeholder track until its song is in; the resource key it takes is in the table below (the steps are in each stage's doc).

Each brief has a style prompt and a lyric field. In the lyric field, everything in square brackets is a direction, not sung. The only
sung lines are wordless ("aah", "oh", "hey"); delete them for a purely instrumental take. Suno takes a lyric field only with Instrumental
off; the style prompt still says no vocals.

### Starfall Causeway — Across the Firmament

Instrumental space-arena combat soundtrack, 140 BPM, an ancient stone bridge suspended in deep space among nebulae and a ringed planet. Cosmic synthwave meets orchestral: driving taiko-style toms with tight electronic drums, deep pulsing sub-bass, shimmering arpeggiated glass synths, a soaring wordless choir pad, heroic brass swells and a memorable five-note celestial bell motif. Vast, majestic, dangerous; builds and releases in 16-bar waves. No vocals. Clean loopable ending.

```
[Intro: lone celestial bells play a five-note motif over a deep space pad, distant wordless choir]

[Build-Up: taiko-style toms enter, pulsing sub-bass, glass arpeggios rising]

[Main Theme: full drums, heroic brass carries the five-note motif, soaring choir]
Aaah, aaah
Aaah, aaah

[Instrumental Break: shimmering arpeggiated glass synths, driving toms, brass stabs]

[Breakdown: drums fall away, only bells, choir and starlight shimmer, held tension]

[Build-Up: snare roll, rising toms, brass swell]

[Climax: full orchestra and synths, choir at full height, the motif in brass and bells together]
Aaah, aaah

[Outro: drums thin back to the intro pulse, bells repeat the motif, seamless loop]

[End]
```

### Substation 128 — Floor Is Lava

Instrumental nightclub combat track, exactly 128 BPM, four-on-the-floor. Punchy festival house and electro in an old power substation: a huge sidechained kick on every beat, rolling offbeat bass, bright supersaw stabs, a catchy chopped vocal-chop style synth lead with no words, crisp claps, rising snare builds into big drops every 16 bars, filter sweeps and laser zaps. Euphoric, neon, relentless, with a clear strong kick for the light show. No vocals. Clean loopable ending.

The club's lights follow the song's kick and bass, and its hazards keep a 128 BPM bar grid on the match clock, so a song at exactly 128 BPM lines the drops up with the stage.

Lyric field:

```
[Intro: filtered four-on-the-floor kick, offbeat hats, electric hum and crackling sparks]

[Build-Up: snare roll, filter sweep opening, laser zaps]

[Drop: huge sidechained kick, rolling offbeat bass, bright supersaw stabs, chopped vocal-chop lead]
(Oh, oh-oh)
(Oh, oh-oh)

[Breakdown: kick drops out, warm pads, the lead melody alone, sub-bass swell]

[Build-Up: rising riser, snare roll doubling, white-noise sweep]

[Drop: bigger and harder, extra supersaw layer, laser zaps on the offbeats]
(Oh, oh-oh)

[Bridge: steady kick with an acid bassline, strobing stabs]

[Build-Up: long riser into the last drop]

[Final Drop: everything at once, euphoric]

[Outro: back to the filtered intro groove, kick steady on every beat for a clean loop]

[End]
```

### Deepwell — The Station Below (`rig-theme`)

Deepwell was rebuilt on 2026-11-11 as a research station on the ocean floor (docs/arenas/DEEPWELL.md). It keeps playing the
owner's `rig-offshore` (Offshore Rig) until a song for the new place is in; that song takes the resource key `rig-theme`.

Aquatic electronica meets orchestral: a heavy four-on-the-floor kick like a piston with a metallic clang on every fourth bar, deep rolling sub-bass, sonar pings, bubbling arpeggiated plucks through a low-pass filter that opens in the builds, warm analog brass pads, bowed vibraphone and glass harmonica, a slow rising whale-song lead on fretless synth. Vast, calm and pressurised, then driving; warm lamps against cold teal. No vocals. Clean loopable ending.

Ooh, ooh
